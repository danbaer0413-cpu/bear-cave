Where the numbers come from

How it works: survey respondents who were confirmed voters (CES) are reweighted so each group's share of voters matches the sliders, then each group's vote is shifted to match its vote slider. Each state's change is added to its certified result; small states lean on the national change. 2000-2012 start from the 2016 survey reshaped to that year's exit poll. Exit polls and the CES measure groups differently (exit polls count more college graduates), so comparing a CES year with an exit-poll year mixes the two. Maine and Nebraska districts aren't split out.

Outside money: super PACs, parties and groups

Independent expenditures reported to the FEC: ad buys, mail and other spending for or against each candidate, made without coordinating with the campaign. Dark money is spending by groups that don't have to name their donors (mostly 501(c)(4) nonprofits and trade associations), plus money those groups give to super PACs. Spending that never names a candidate, or runs outside the pre-election windows, isn't reported to the FEC at all; TV stations' political files and the Meta and Google ad libraries would be the next sources for it.

Biggest donors

The largest donors to each finalist's campaign, people and PACs listed separately, and who funds the outside groups spending most in the race. Individuals can give a campaign at most $3,500 per election (primary and general count separately); super PACs can take unlimited amounts, so their donor lists are where the biggest checks show up.
